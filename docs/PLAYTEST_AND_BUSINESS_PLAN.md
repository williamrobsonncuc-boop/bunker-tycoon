# Haven 17 · Playtest and business plan

This document turns the creative direction into evidence-led production work. It is a plan, not a prediction of revenue.

## Signature promise

Haven 17 is a civilian World War 3 bunker management game about making promises a shelter can actually keep. The player builds rooms, staffs systems, decides who to admit and records what happens to the community.

The game should be recognisable in one sentence:

> Build a government emergency shelter beneath a broken city, then decide what kind of community can survive there.

## First playable chapter

**The First Blackout** is the opening chapter.

The player should be able to:

1. Understand the shelter map and build one room.
2. Assign a resident to a job.
3. Make the first admission decision.
4. Run an operation and read its consequence.
5. Build medical capacity.
6. Reach a community of eight and file the chapter report.

The chapter should take roughly 15–25 minutes in a tuned build. Do not expand the campaign until independent players can finish this route without developer help.

## Playtest sequence

### Round one: usability

Watch ten people who have not seen the code or design brief. Give them only the game and ask them to think aloud. Record:

- Whether they understand what the central shaft means.
- Whether they can explain why a room is not producing.
- Whether they read the admission forecast.
- Whether they understand the daily drop without being told to return compulsively.
- Whether they can name one resident after playing.

Do not rescue players immediately. Note the moment and exact wording of confusion.

### Round two: emotional and strategic value

Give a new group the chapter. Ask after play:

- Which decision felt most meaningful?
- Which resident or event do they remember?
- Did they feel pressured, bored or lost?
- What did they expect the next chapter to contain?
- Would they recommend the game, and what would they describe it as?

Treat answers as qualitative evidence, not a market forecast.

### Round three: controlled balance

Compare two tuned versions with the same art and onboarding. Change only one variable at a time: build costs, arrival spacing, operation cooldowns or chapter length. Use a stable build identifier and keep notes on device, session length and completion.

Useful internal targets:

- 8/10 new players complete the core construction/admission flow without intervention.
- 7/10 can explain at least one resource trade-off.
- 6/10 remember a resident or story detail the following day.
- No critical save-loss or unrecoverable state is observed.

These are working targets, not guarantees of retention or commercial success.

## Brand direction

The brand is a restrained civil-defence archive rather than a cartoon apocalypse.

- Palette: concrete grey, paper white, charcoal ink, olive/steel and warning amber.
- Typography: official-looking condensed or monospaced labels paired with a highly readable humanist body face.
- Shape language: rounded enough for mobile touch, but based on signage, folders, stamped forms and infrastructure.
- Voice: calm, humane, practical and specific. Avoid jokes about real-world suffering.
- Repeated motifs: Haven 17 stencil, Lantern Programme seals, numbered rooms, redacted notices and hand-written community notes.

Every new asset should answer: does this look like it belongs to the same government emergency programme?

## Fair commercial model

The recommended model is:

- Free opening chapter.
- One transparent, reasonably priced campaign unlock.
- Optional cosmetic packs: signage colours, wall finishes, room plaques and archive covers.
- No paid food, water, medicine or “keep your settlement alive” skips.
- No premium randomised rewards.
- Daily gifts remain optional and modest.

Before launch, confirm the current Apple and Google platform policies, age rating, privacy disclosures, purchase restoration and consumer-law requirements. Do not advertise gameplay or rewards that are not in the shipped build.

## Release gates

Do not call the game production-ready until:

- The first chapter is completable from a fresh save.
- Saves survive reload, backgrounding and import/export.
- A physical-phone pass checks touch targets, text scaling, battery use and performance.
- Store screenshots match the shipped build.
- The name and logo have been checked for trademark conflicts.
- The purchase flow is tested in a sandbox and restores correctly.
- Support contact, privacy information and known limitations are ready.

## What not to build yet

Defer multiplayer, PvP, chat, player markets, large surface combat, blockchain, dozens of currencies, procedural everything and a large backend. These increase support and production cost before the signature experience has evidence.
